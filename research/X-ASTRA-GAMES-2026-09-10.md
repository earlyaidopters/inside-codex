# Astra games: positive, critical and mixed X conversations

Research date: September 10, 2026. Used Mark's signed-in X account and SuperGrok in Codex's internal browser. Two research prompts sought balanced original threads, then educational/creative-value defenses and precise critical replies. Original posts and visible replies below were independently opened and read in that browser. No public posts, replies or reactions were made. Games and codebases were not independently playtested in this research pass.

Grok conversation: https://x.com/i/grok?conversation=2098185358949396527

## Findings

The verified examples contain real enthusiasm about creation, graphics, working browser projects and education. Criticism concerns gameplay depth, inflated one-shot language, quota use and device performance. Some of the strongest qualifications come from the builders themselves. These posts do not establish population sentiment, sustained player retention, learning gains or economic outcomes.

The most useful comparison for Inside Codex is Georgia Witchel's chip-design learning game. It establishes an existing educational direction, not that Mark is first or that either game improves learning. Rishi's defense of expression and joy also challenges any claim that games without revenue or instruction are inherently worthless.

## Implication for Mark's project

The verified discussion supports a tension between impressive creation and a satisfying, useful experience. It also supplies positive educational examples and an explicit defense of making for enjoyment. A defensible future comparison can ask what happens when the builder defines a practical learning purpose, adds meaningful exercises and tests the delivered result. It cannot yet claim that other games have no value, that nobody plays them, that Inside Codex is the first educational Astra game, or that it has proven learning outcomes.
